# When to Call the Doctor: Postpartum Warning Signs Every New Mom Should Know

The standard of care sends new mothers home 48 hours after delivery with a pamphlet and a six-week follow-up appointment. The riskiest stretch of recovery — the first two weeks — sits in between, when the most common and dangerous complications peak.

This guide covers the warning signs that warrant a call to your provider. Not every symptom means something is wrong, but knowing which ones to take seriously could save your life.

---

## Postpartum Hemorrhage (Heavy Bleeding)

Some bleeding after delivery is normal (lochia). It should gradually taper from bright red to pink to brown over 2-6 weeks.

**Call your provider immediately if:**
- You soak a pad in less than one hour
- You pass clots larger than a golf ball
- Bleeding that had slowed down suddenly becomes heavy again
- You feel dizzy, lightheaded, or faint

**Why this matters:** Postpartum hemorrhage is the leading cause of maternal mortality in the first week after birth. It's treatable, but every hour counts.

---

## Infection (Endometritis, Wound Infection, UTI)

Infection is the second leading cause of postpartum maternal death. It can occur in the uterus (endometritis), at the C-section or episiotomy site, or in the urinary tract.

**Call your provider if:**
- Fever over 100.4°F (38°C)
- Chills or body aches
- Foul-smelling vaginal discharge
- Pain or burning with urination
- Incision (C-section or perineal) that is red, warm, swollen, or draining fluid
- Increasing pelvic pain instead of decreasing

**Why this matters:** Postpartum infections can progress to sepsis within hours. Antibiotics are highly effective when started early.

---

## Preeclampsia / Hypertension

Preeclampsia can develop during pregnancy AND in the first 6 weeks postpartum. Many women are no longer monitoring their blood pressure after delivery.

**Call your provider if:**
- Severe headache that doesn't respond to pain medication
- Vision changes (blurring, seeing spots, sensitivity to light)
- Swelling in your hands, face, or legs that suddenly gets worse
- Shortness of breath
- Upper abdominal pain (under the ribs), especially on the right
- Nausea or vomiting (new, not related to a stomach bug)

**Why this matters:** Postpartum preeclampsia can lead to seizure (eclampsia) or stroke. It's fully treatable but requires prompt medical attention.

---

## Venous Thromboembolism (Blood Clots)

Pregnancy and the postpartum period increase your risk of blood clots by 5-10x. C-section further increases this risk.

**Call your provider or go to the ER if:**
- One leg is swollen, warm, or painful (especially the calf or thigh)
- The skin on your leg is red or discolored
- **Sudden shortness of breath**
- **Chest pain, especially when breathing deeply**
- **Coughing up blood**

**Why this matters:** A clot that travels to your lungs (pulmonary embolism) is a leading cause of postpartum death. If you have shortness of breath or chest pain, go to the ER — do not wait for an office visit.

---

## Postpartum Mental Health Emergencies

Postpartum depression and anxiety affect 1 in 7 mothers. Postpartum psychosis is rare (1-2 per 1,000 births) but is a medical emergency.

**Call your provider or a crisis line if:**
- You feel sad, empty, or hopeless for more than two weeks
- You can't sleep when the baby sleeps (racing thoughts, anxiety)
- You're having intrusive thoughts (images or urges that scare you)
- You feel like you're "going crazy"
- You have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby

**Crisis resources:**
- **National Maternal Mental Health Hotline:** 1-833-943-5746 (24/7, free, confidential)
- **Postpartum Support International:** 1-800-944-4773
- **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline:** Call or text 988

**Why this matters:** Perinatal mood and anxiety disorders are treatable. You are not a bad mother for having these thoughts. Your brain is going through a massive hormonal shift, and sometimes it needs medical help to rebalance.

---

## When in Doubt, Call

Here's the most important thing to know: **you will not be wasting anyone's time.**

OB practices would rather see 100 worried mothers with nothing wrong than miss one with a treatable complication. The triage nurse's job is to help you figure out what's urgent and what can wait.

**When in doubt:**
- Call your OB's after-hours line
- Go to urgent care if you can't reach anyone
- Go to the ER for: chest pain, shortness of breath, seizure, heavy bleeding, suicidal thoughts

---

## What to Say When You Call

"I delivered [vaginal/C-section] on [date]. I'm [X days/weeks] postpartum. I'm experiencing [symptom]. It started [when]. On a scale of 1-10, it's a [number]. I'm also [any other symptoms]."

This gives the triage nurse everything they need to assess urgency.

---

## The Bottom Line

The first six weeks are the highest-risk period of your recovery. Your body is doing something extraordinary, and it needs monitoring.

You are not overreacting. You are not being dramatic. You are protecting yourself so you can be there for your baby.
