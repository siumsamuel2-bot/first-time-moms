# Preparing for Baby #2: What Nobody Tells You About the Transition

Going from one to two children is not "one more." It's a complete restructuring of your family system. Here's what to actually expect and how to prepare.

## The Hardest Part Nobody Warns You About

**The betrayal your firstborn feels is real.** They didn't choose this. One day they had your undivided attention; the next day they have to wait while you feed, change, and soothe someone else.

This guilt is normal. It doesn't mean you're doing something wrong.

## Practical Preparation

### Set Up Before Baby Arrives
- **Toddler-proof the nursery.** Your older child will want to "help." Remove choking hazards from reach, secure furniture, and create a designated spot for them in the baby's room.
- **Practice the shift before the birth.** Have your partner or support person take over primary toddler duty for several days before your due date. You'll both be better prepared.

### The First Weeks
- **Lower your standards.** If the toddler watches more TV than you'd like for the first 6 weeks, no one is keeping score. Survival mode is temporary.
- **Keep a special "big sibling" basket.** When you're nursing, have a basket of special toys or activities that ONLY come out during nursing sessions.

## The Emotional Transition

### For the Older Child
- Give them jobs. "Can you bring me a diaper?" gives them ownership over their role as big sibling.
- Name their feelings. "It's hard to wait when Mama is feeding the baby. I see you're feeling impatient."

### For the Parents
- Your capacity doesn't double. It actually decreases because your attention is split.
- Protect one-on-one time with each child, even if it's 10 minutes.
- Accept that someone is always going to need something. That's not failure — that's family life with two under two (or two under five).

## When to Use Firstweeks

The postpartum period after baby #2 or #3 is actually higher risk for mood disorders than the first. You have less time to monitor yourself, more demands on your attention, and often less support.

Use Firstweeks to:
- Track your mood daily (it takes 30 seconds)
- Monitor for PPD/PPA symptoms you might miss
- Find a provider who understands the unique challenges of parenting multiple children

## The Bottom Line

The transition from one to two is hard — harder than zero to one for many parents. But the love multiplies, and eventually, so does the ease. You've got this.
