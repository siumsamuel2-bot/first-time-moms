# Returning to Work After Baby: A Practical Guide for New Moms

The transition back to work is one of the most emotionally complex moments of early motherhood. Whether you're returning at 6 weeks, 12 weeks, or later, the mix of relief, guilt, anxiety, and hope is normal — even when it doesn't feel like it.

## Before You Go Back

### 1. Do a trial run
A week before your start date, run through the full morning routine: wake up, get baby ready, pack pump/bottles, and get out the door by your target time. The first time shouldn't be the first day.

### 2. Set boundaries in advance
Send a brief email to your manager and team before your return:

> "I'm looking forward to being back on [date]. I'll be protecting [time block] for pumping and will block it on my calendar. I may need flexibility for pediatrician visits during the first few weeks."

You set the tone for what you need. Most managers are happy to support when they know what to expect.

### 3. Practice the pump-and-work rhythm
If you're nursing, figure out your pumping setup before day one. Block your calendar. Test your pump at your desk. Know where the mother's room is.

## The First Week Back

**It will be hard. That's not a sign you're doing it wrong.**

- Plan for half-days if possible — a full 8-hour day after weeks of maternity leave is a shock to your system
- Give yourself permission to look at baby photos. Looking at your baby triggers oxytocin release, which helps with letdown and mood regulation
- Have a cry kit in your bag — tissues, a comforting item, a note from your partner

## Managing Pumping at Work

- Know your rights: the PUMP Act provides break time and private space (not a bathroom) for pumping
- Hands-free pumping bras are worth the investment
- A mini fridge under your desk is a game-changer
- Label everything. Assume someone will open the shared fridge

## What Nobody Tells You

- **The grief is real.** You're not wrong to feel like you're leaving a part of yourself at home. That's attachment, and it's healthy.
- **Your priorities will shift.** Work that felt urgent before may feel trivial now. That's also normal. It recalibrates over time.
- **You're more efficient than before.** Moms returning to work are often more focused and productive because they have to be. Use your time intentionally.

## When to Use Firstweeks

The transition back to work is a high-risk period for postpartum mood disorders. If you're tracking:
- Persistent sadness lasting more than 2 weeks after returning
- Anxiety about baby's safety while at work that interferes with function
- Difficulty bonding when you're home

Firstweeks can help identify escalation patterns and connect you to a provider.

## Resources

- **The PUMP Act**: Break time and space for nursing mothers at work
- **Return-to-work coaching**: Many employers offer this through EAP
- **New mom groups at work**: Check if your company has a parents' ERG
