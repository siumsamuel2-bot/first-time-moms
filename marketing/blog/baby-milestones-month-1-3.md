# Baby Milestones Month 1-3: What to Expect and When to Ask for Help

Every baby develops at their own pace. But knowing the general range for key milestones helps you celebrate progress and recognize when something deserves a conversation with your pediatrician.

## Month 1: The Fourth Trimester

### What Most Babies Do
- Lift head briefly during tummy time
- Focus on faces 8-12 inches away
- React to sound (startle, blink, quiet)
- Make throaty sounds (not quite coos yet)
- Track objects with eyes (briefly)

### Red Flags to Discuss With Your Pediatrician
- Not waking for feeds
- Stiff or floppy body tone
- No response to loud sounds

Parenting tip: Month 1 is about survival, not achievement. If baby is eating and gaining weight, you're both doing great.

## Month 2: Emerging Awareness

### What Most Babies Do
- Smile socially (not just gas)
- Coo and make vowel sounds
- Follow faces and objects more deliberately
- Hold head up for short periods
- Start to prefer parents and familiar caregivers

### Red Flags
- No social smile by 8 weeks
- Not tracking faces
- Excessive fussiness that doesn't respond to soothing

Month 2 is often when postpartum mood disorders peak. If you're not feeling the joy everyone promised, use Firstweeks to track your mood and get support.

## Month 3: Getting Interactive

### What Most Babies Do
- Hold head steady when supported
- Reach for and bat at objects
- Bring hands to mouth
- Recognize familiar faces at a distance
- Laugh out loud

### Red Flags
- No cooing or babbling
- Not reaching for objects
- Persistent fisting of hands after 3 months

## When Milestones Are Delayed

Early intervention is most effective when started early. If you're concerned:

1. **Track what you see.** Firstweeks can log daily observations and flag patterns.
2. **Trust your gut.** Parents are right about developmental concerns more often than not.
3. **Talk to your pediatrician.** Bring specific examples: "She's not reaching for toys" vs. "I think she's behind."

## What NOT to Worry About

- Rolling over: Some babies roll at 2 months; others wait until 5 months. Both are normal.
- Sttn: "Sleeping through the night" at 3 months means a 5-6 hour stretch, not 12 hours.
- Growth percentiles: A baby in the 5th percentile who follows their curve is just as healthy as a baby in the 95th percentile.

## The Takeaway

Milestones are ranges, not deadlines. Your baby's job in the first 3 months is to eat, grow, and bond with you. Everything else is a bonus.
