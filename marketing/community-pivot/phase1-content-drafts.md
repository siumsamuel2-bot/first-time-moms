### Reddit — r/beyondthebump

**Title:** What I wish someone had told me about week 2 postpartum

**Body:**
No one prepares you for the emotional drop after the first week. The adrenaline wears off, the visitors stop coming, and you're suddenly alone with this tiny human and a thousand questions.

Here's what I learned the hard way:

1. **The "baby blues" peak at day 5, not day 2.** I spent days 3-4 thinking I was fine, then day 5 hit me like a truck. Crying for no reason. Feeling overwhelmed by a onesie. If that's you right now — it's normal and it passes.

2. **Your milk coming in is a whole event.** I knew it would happen. I did not know I'd feel like I had two angry rocks on my chest. Heat packs, cold packs, nursing bra that actually fits — get them ready.

3. **Everyone asks about the baby. Almost no one asks about you.** Schedule one thing for yourself each day, even if it's just 5 minutes to drink coffee while it's still hot. Your recovery matters too.

4. **The "is this normal?" spiral is real.** I Googled "newborn breathing" at 3 AM more times than I can count. You're not crazy. You're a new mom. Those are different things.

What do you wish someone had told you about those early weeks?

### Reddit — r/BabyBumps

**Title:** Third trimester prep I actually used postpartum

**Body:**
Everyone tells you to prep for labor. No one tells you to prep for what comes after. Here are 5 things that actually saved me in the first two weeks:

1. **Freezer meals, but not the casserole kind.** Make things you can eat one-handed. Burritos, muffins, protein balls. Nothing that requires a knife and fork while holding a sleeping baby.

2. **A "recovery station" in every room.** Diapers, wipes, a change of clothes, snacks, water bottle — have a basket in the living room, bedroom, and nursery. You will not want to walk across the house at 2 AM.

3. **Know the difference between baby blues and PPD.** Baby blues: crying, mood swings, anxiety that comes and goes, resolves by week 2. PPD: persistent sadness, loss of interest, changes in sleep/appetite that last beyond 2 weeks. Both are real. Both deserve support.

4. **Pelvic floor PT is not optional.** Whether you had a vaginal birth or C-section, your pelvic floor just went through trauma. Get a referral. Go to the appointments. You'll thank yourself at 6 weeks.

5. **Build your support script now.** "Can you bring dinner?" "Can you hold the baby so I can shower?" "I need to rest, please text before coming over." Practice saying these things. Your people want to help. Tell them how.

Current third trimester moms — what are you doing to prepare for the fourth trimester?

### Brand Compliance Checklist
Before posting any consumer-facing copy:
- [ ] Leads with empathy, not fear
- [ ] Uses plain English — no jargon without translation
- [ ] "Postpartum" (not "postnatal")
- [ ] "Feeding your baby" (not breastfeeding by default)
- [ ] Contractions present (you're, don't, we'll)
- [ ] No scare tactics, no medical diagnosis claims
- [ ] Calls out "flag" / "alert" — not "diagnose" / "detect"
- [ ] Second person ("you") throughout
- [ ] Matches channel-specific tone per Brand Voice Guide

### Escalation Notes
- Reddit posts should be posted from personal accounts, not corporate accounts
- Facebook group posts need admin approval in private groups
- TikTok and Instagram can use the Firstweeks handle once created
- Track all posting in engagement tracker spreadsheet
